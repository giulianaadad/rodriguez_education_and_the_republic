This section of the khipu shows that the Incas grouped related information together in clusters. In the image, several cords hang closely together beneath the end of the primary cord, forming tight clusters of pendant and subsidiary strands. Garcilaso notes that each thread represented a specific subject and that cords were arranged in a meaningful order (p. 330). The visual clustering indicates that these cords belong to the same entry or category. Each pendant adds one detail, and together they form a complete record within the larger system. Although the material format changed after colonization, the practice of grouping related information continued in tables, lists, and columns.
