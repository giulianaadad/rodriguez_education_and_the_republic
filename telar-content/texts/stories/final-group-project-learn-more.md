Guamán Poma was writing ultimately for King Phillip III, as Bauer describes the manuscript as a "Written in the form of a petition to Philip III, his chronicle exposes the abuses of the recent colonial authorities" (Bauer 281). This petition is intended for a devout Catholic king, and as Poma describes in his words: "no written records except for the quips and the memories and accounts of some old Indias" (Bauer 284). He draws on the mestizo sancto framework, the"generic conventions of the Spanish Vita" because he himself identifies with Catholicism (278, Bauer).
