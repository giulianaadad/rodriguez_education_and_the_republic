title: "Learn more"
---

The darker brown knots in this section highlight how color once played a role in distinguishing meaning within the khipu. Garcilaso explains that "they were threaded in order on a longer string like a fringe (pg. 330)". The colors usually showed what subject the thread was about, such as "yellow for gold, white for silver, and red for warriors (pg.330)." The cords in this khipu fall into a deeper brown that does not appear in his examples, which means its specific meaning cannot be fully recovered today. This gap shows how certain parts of the Inca record keeping system were forgotten when alphabetic writing replaced Indigenous methods. Even though the meaning of this darker shade has been lost, the physical organization of the cords still reveals continuity in how information was grouped and structured. The color may no longer be readable, but the system that produced it remains visible.

Reference:
Garcilaso de la Vega. "Posts, Relays, and the Quipus." Royal Commentaries, bk. 6, chs. 7 – 9, pp. 328 – 333.
