---
title: "The quipucamayus and their role in the Inca society"
---

The quipucamayus were trained specialists responsible for tying, reading, and preserving the information stored in the cords. Their work kept the system functioning and ensured that records were accurate and shared across communities. That said, the Inca republic relied on quipucamayus in order to function. When the Spanish arrived, the functionality of their system changed, but the main source of information continues to be the khipu. Garcilaso mentions that "they all kept the same records, and although one accountant or scribe was all that would have been necessary to keep them, the Inca preferred to have plenty in each village and for each sort of calculation, so as to avoid faults that might occur if there were few" (p. 329).The number of quipucamayus maintained across villages shows how essential they were to the Inca republic. Their presence ensured accuracy and accountability in every record. The visual of a modern researcher studying the same cords centuries later demonstrates continuity because even though the Andes have changed, the khipu remains a living method for exploring record keeping and the people who once created these stories.

Reference:
Garcilaso de la Vega. "Posts, Relays, and the Quipus." Royal Commentaries, bk. 6, chs. 7 – 9, pp. 328 – 333.
