---
title: "The Khipu as a Record Keeping Tool in the Pre-Colonial Andes"
---

The Khipu was a type of communication that shows the Inca had their own means of collecting and organizing information. It served as a pillar of Inca society, functioning as the main system of record keeping before the Spanish arrived in the Andes. The full khipu shows that the Incas kept records by spreading information across many cords attached to one main cord. In the image, dozens of pendant cords hang in an orderly line from a single long primary cord, each holding data through knots, placement, and color. Garcilaso explains that the Khipu was a system of cyphers by which "the Inca and his governors agreed on what was to be done, and the knots and colors implied things such as the number of men, arms, or clothes, or supplies or whatever it was that had to be made or sent or prepared" (p. 329). The visual layout reflects the organized structure of Inca society, showing how separate strands formed one unified record.

Reference:
Garcilaso de la Vega. "Posts, Relays, and the Quipus." Royal Commentaries, bk. 6, chs. 7 – 9, pp. 328 – 333.
