title: "Learn more"
---

The Incas only had the khipu and no knowledge of alphabetic writing, so they used whatever devices they could to preserve history and memory. The khipu was the writing system of their republic, a tool that preserved the essence of their society. As Garcilaso explains, "All these forms were perishable expedients, for it is letters that perpetuate the memory of events" (p. 332). Those who did not learn the meaning of the cords through memory or tradition were as unaware of their content as a foreigner (p. 333). When alphabetic writing replaced the khipu in the colonial period, the form of writing changed, but the object remains as evidence of a resilient society that resisted erasure and as a testament to a republic once united by one tool: the khipu.

Reference:
Garcilaso de la Vega. "Posts, Relays, and the Quipus." Royal Commentaries, bk. 6, chs. 7 – 9, pp. 328 – 333.
