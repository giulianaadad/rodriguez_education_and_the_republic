The picture is a visual poem called "Impression of Havana" by Jose Tablada. Instead of writing the poem in normal lines, the words are placed in different directions and shapes, which makes the poem look like a picture. The poem is about Havana and includes things like the water palm trees, lights, and Christopher Columbus. The different shapes and placement of the words make the reader look at the poem in a different way instead of just reading from left to right. I think the image is showing how Havana has many different parts that come together to create one place. It also connects to change and continuity because the poem uses a new style of writing while still talking about the history and culture of Havana. Anyways, the picture makes the poem more interesting because you can understand the meaning through both the words and the way they look. 
Work Cited:
Tablada, Jose Juan. "Impression of Havana." 1918. Vanguardia y concretismo.
