The knots on each cord show how the Incas used a positional counting system to organize numerical information. Garcilaso explains that knots were arranged in order of units, tens, hundreds, and thousands, and that they never exceed nine within each position (p. 330). In this cluster, each cord carries a different number of knots, creating a visible pattern of values that allows the Incas to track information with precision. This system was powerful for recording numbers, but it could not express spoken words or full narratives. Everything that could not be tied into knots had to be preserved through memory, passed down by quipucamayus and amautas, while poets created short verses to keep fragments of history alive. When written numbers replaced knots during the colonial period, the khipu recording method was nearly forgotten and its continuity was disrupted. What remains today is the structure of the counting system, but not the stories that once accompanied it.

Reference:
Garcilaso de la Vega. "Posts, Relays, and the Quipus." Royal Commentaries, bk. 6, chs. 7 – 9, pp. 328 – 333.
